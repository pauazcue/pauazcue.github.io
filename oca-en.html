The context

A transformation process that was just starting to take shape.

When I joined OCA, the company was taking its first steps into a digital transformation process. The Product team was just starting to form, and there was no Product Design practice integrated into product development yet.

We found multiple platforms built at different times, with different technologies, design criteria and business rules. Every new requirement responded to a one-off need, which generated inconsistent experiences, duplicated functionality and technical debt that made evolving the products increasingly difficult.

The challenge wasn't redesigning an application. The challenge was helping build a different way of designing digital products inside a large-scale organization.

My role

From interface design to building a team and a practice.

I started as a Product Designer and grew alongside the team as it took shape. I drove user interviews, discovery sessions, workshops with different areas and new collaborative ways of working to bring research into the design process.

I also participated in creating the organization's first shared Figma file and building a common base to unify criteria across products. Over time, I helped build a team that today works autonomously and keeps evolving those practices.

My work wasn't limited to interface design. I looked to connect business, technology and users so product decisions were backed by context, evidence and a shared vision.

How I worked

Most of the work happened outside of Figma.

I interviewed clients, visited logistics plants, observed operational processes, worked alongside commercial teams and facilitated workshops with different areas to understand how products actually worked and spot opportunities for improvement.

One of the challenges was building trust around research. In many cases, it was the first time a Product team observed the day-to-day work of operators and users. Over time, those sessions became a tool to align decisions, validate hypotheses and bring different areas of the organization closer together.
