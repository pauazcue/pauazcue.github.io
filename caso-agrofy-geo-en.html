Agrofy 2021

Geo — Geolocation

A buyer in Buenos Aires shouldn't find out on the product detail page that freight makes the purchase unfeasible. Agrofy connected buyers and sellers across four countries — but didn't show distance. That created a silent problem with real consequences.

Senior Product Designer

Context

Latin America's largest agro marketplace had an invisible problem.

Agrofy operated in four countries, connecting buyers and sellers of agricultural machinery, seeds, supplies and services. In this market, a product's origin isn't a secondary detail — it's a variable that can make a purchase unfeasible. A harvester in Santa Fe isn't the same as one in Formosa.

The outcome was predictable: buyers would reach the detail page, see the seller's location, calculate the freight and leave. No conversion. On the other side, sellers were getting inquiries from buyers who could never actually buy from them — leads with no real value.

"They call me from 800 km away to ask the price. When I tell them the freight cost, they hang up. Those leads are worthless."

Discovery

The metrics showed the bounce. Sellers explained why.

The metrics showed the bounce but not the why. The first step was talking directly with the platform's sellers — the ones most affected by low-quality leads.

We also interviewed buyers and observed search patterns. Benchmarking similar platforms confirmed that distance and location were present in most competitors, especially for high-value, hard-to-ship products.

The process

End-to-end design: from search to seller contact.

01

Mapping edge cases

We mapped every possible scenario: buyers with and without location enabled, anonymous vs. logged-in users, results inside and outside the relevant radius, behavior by product type. Each case with its own specific solution.

02

Designing the home and listing flow

We added the option to enter or detect location from the home page, propagating through listing and detail. Users could search "tractors near Rosario" and see results ordered by distance.

03

Redesigning the product detail

On the detail page, distance to the seller became a prominent data point, alongside a freight estimate whenever it was possible to calculate.

04

CEO validation and a staged MVP

Given the impact on the leads model, we presented the design to the CEO and Head of Product. The rollout was progressive: first Argentina, then Brazil, Uruguay and Colombia.

The impact

Less bounce, better leads, more trust in the platform.

Surfacing distance as a visible variable from the start of the journey reduced bounce on the product detail page, especially in heavy machinery categories where freight is a bigger deciding factor.

On the sellers' side, better lead quality was the most valued change: contacts arriving had higher real purchase intent, since they already knew where the product was before starting the conversation.
