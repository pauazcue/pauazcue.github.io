Agrofy 2021–2023

Listing — catalog, filters and trade show research

To improve the listing of an agro marketplace, we first had to learn how a farmer actually buys. Agrofy had thousands of products — but CTR to the detail page was low and merchants weren't publishing prices. Before designing solutions, we needed to understand the domain from the inside.

Role
Senior Product Designer
Period
2021–2023
Scope
Discovery · Listing · Filters · Field research

The problem

Users filtered a lot — but weren't reaching the product detail.

The metrics showed something contradictory: filter usage was healthy, but CTR to the detail page was low and there was little movement on the listing. Users searched, filtered and left.

Auditing the filters during the listing migration, we found an accumulation of problems: the most-used filters were at the bottom of the list, you couldn't select more than one value, some filters were conceptually redundant, and filters with no results stayed visible, generating clicks that led nowhere.

On top of that, most merchants didn't publish prices. We needed alternative strategies to raise CTR in that context.

We set out to understand how they made decisions, how they searched, how they paid, how they chose one product over another. And whether they needed to see the product before buying it.

Discovery and research

We didn't know the domain. Before designing, we had to learn how the field buys.

As a UX team, we had no knowledge of the agricultural world. The real challenge was competing with the physical purchase: farmers were used to going to a local distributor, seeing the product and negotiating face to face.

01

Surveys and user interviews

Unexpected finding: many of the contacts weren't farmers at all, but people looking for deals who had landed on Agrofy by chance. That changed how we segmented the insights.

02

Trade show research — Expoagro and Agroactiva

We went to the aisles of Argentina's two biggest agricultural trade shows to talk to real buyers in context: how they normally shopped, what sites they checked online, how they went about buying used machinery.

03

Heuristic analysis of the existing listing

Systematic evaluation focused on system visibility, error prevention and consistency. The problems we found were systemic — they couldn't be fixed with one-off tweaks.

04

Benchmark: agro + world-class e-commerce

Two tracks: agro-domain platforms (MercadoLibre, John Deere, Tienda Luisa Brazil) and leading marketplaces (Amazon, Alibaba, Airbnb) to bring in the best experience standards regardless of industry.

Design decisions

Fewer options at first glance, more decision-making power.

→

Filter architecture based on real usage

Most-used filters first. Conceptual duplicates merged. Filters with no results hidden. Multi-select added. Quick filters by category.

→

From 5 cards to 3 per row, with more visible information

Fewer products per row but more relevant information visible on each before opening the detail — applying Hick's Law to reduce cognitive load.

→

Minimal, consistent design

Reduced visual noise, clearer information hierarchy and consistent patterns across categories.

→

A strategy for missing prices

Alternative signals of value: seller relevance, number of listings, highlighted attributes and geographic proximity as trust indicators when there was no visible price.

Impact

More filter usage, more leads, better conversion.

Sessions with filter usage convert almost three times more than sessions without filters — driving more traffic into that segment has a direct impact on business results.
