The transformation of OCA's flagship corporate shipping platform

ePak is the platform OCA's corporate clients use to manage their shipments. Over more than 30 years, the product grew incrementally from one-off business needs, without a unified product strategy.

The result was a platform critical to the operation, but complex, inconsistent and hard to scale.

Context

An aging platform. A client who uses it every day.

ePak is the shipping platform used by OCA's corporate clients. After more than 30 years of accumulated technical debt, the product had lost consistency: duplicated functionality, confusing flows and a manual of more than 70 pages to complete everyday tasks. Queries to the Customer Experience team were constant, and the platform could no longer keep pace with the business's growth.

Despite that, it remained a critical piece of the operation. More than 5,000 corporate clients use it every day to create around 1 million shipments a month.

Challenge

A platform central to the operation — with structural limits.

ePak was a platform central to OCA's operation, but its historical evolution had created friction in use and structural limitations:

01

It required training to operate

It wasn't an intuitive tool. Using it well depended on prior knowledge passed from person to person.

02

Users relied on a manual of more than 70 pages

The documentation wasn't a nice-to-have — it was a requirement to operate without making mistakes.

03

Flows were fragmented across multiple tools

A single task could require jumping between different systems to complete.

04

Information was inconsistent and hard to interpret

The real state of the operation wasn't always visible or reliable from the platform.

05

The architecture wasn't ready for new products

Every new feature built on the existing base meant, every time, one more patch on top of another.

The challenge wasn't improving an interface — it was building a foundation that would let the product evolve sustainably.

My role as Product Designer

I led the discovery and a full understanding of ePak.

We introduced a way of working that hadn't existed before in OCA's digital product development. We interviewed corporate clients, worked with the commercial team to get access to users, brought in Customer Experience, Operations Engineering and Systems, built blueprints and analyzed the operation end to end.

This approach let us stop prioritizing initiatives based purely on one-off requirements and start making decisions backed by research, evidence and real business needs.

My role as Design Lead

Months later I took over leadership of the design team.

I defined the product's design strategy, coordinated the team and guided ePak's evolution, keeping decisions consistent as the platform gained new capabilities and prepared to grow alongside OCA's digital ecosystem.

Discovery

Understanding the old platform before designing the new one.

I made the call not to propose solutions until we had a complete picture of the ecosystem: what the current platform actually did, how clients really used it, what they asked the commercial team for, and how what they entered into ePak later impacted the operation.

01

Audit of the existing platform

Full mapping of the current architecture: which features existed, which were actually used, which caused confusion or were duplicated. The starting point was understanding what to keep, what to remove and what to improve — before proposing anything new.

02

Full service blueprint

Mapped the interaction between clients, operations, systems and CX to build a shared view of the entire ecosystem. The blueprint was the first time every team saw the full end-to-end journey.

03

Workshops with internal teams

Sessions with Commercial, Systems, Operations and CX to understand constraints, dependencies and business goals. Each area only had a partial view of the problem.

04

Interviews with users and the commercial team

Understanding real needs: what tasks people did every day, what failed them, what they asked the commercial team for that the platform didn't give them. Reviewing incidents and frequent inquiries mapped the real pain points — not assumptions.

05

Analysis of communications and flows

Documented touchpoints, transactional emails and shipment status updates. The experience problems weren't only on screen — they also lived in the communications clients received after operating.

06

AI to speed up the process

Used Claude to synthesize interviews, analyze findings and write documentation — bringing AI into the team as a real working tool.

The first goal wasn't to design a new platform. It was to understand why the current one existed.

Design

A working tool, not a new interface.

The goal was never to make a prettier platform. It was to make a platform that solved the daily problems of the people working with it — and that no longer needed a 70-page manual to be usable.

We worked through the critical journeys: creating shipments, tracking, history and proof of delivery. Every decision was validated before going into development.

→

From form to flow

Creating a shipment used to be a long form with many fields and several failure points. We turned it into a step-by-step guided flow — easier to complete, easier to correct, with better data quality on entry.

→

Client data affects the operation

A finding that shifted the project's focus: what the client enters into ePak — weights, dimensions, packaging, destination info, declared value — directly impacts the operation and the delivery experience. Improving that data's quality wasn't just UX — it meant reducing operational errors across the whole chain.

→

Proof of delivery, on the platform

Clients used to call CX to request it. Drivers logged it in their own app (Driving). Connecting that data and showing it directly on the shipment detail eliminated one of the most frequent reasons for contact — and gave clients back their autonomy.

→

Information to share, not just to view

Clients needed to share shipment status with their own customers. List downloads, real-time status, exportable data — the platform went from an internal panel to a tool clients used in front of their own customers.

Users didn't need to learn how OCA worked. They needed to understand what was happening with their shipments.

The transformation

The project redefined what ePak was for.

→

From operational tool to management platform

ePak went from a tool focused solely on creating shipments to a platform where clients manage their entire operation.

→

Unifying the ecosystem

We centralized functionality that used to require separate tools, simplifying the experience and reducing fragmented workflows.

→

Visibility into the operation

We redesigned how clients access shipment information, improving their understanding of the operation and their autonomy.

→

A platform built to evolve

We designed a flexible architecture to add new modules, services and products without repeating the limitations of the past.

The design system built in parallel — Ñandú — was the infrastructure that made it possible to scale consistency across ePak and OCA's other products.

Impact

Transforming a platform and how products get built.

Beyond the interface improvements, ePak installed a way of working at OCA: with research, early validation and a product vision that hadn't existed before.

→

30% fewer Customer Experience queries related to using the platform (−50% in tracking queries, −30% in proof-of-delivery queries).

→

Eliminated the operating manual as a training requirement.

→

Introduced product analytics to support new decisions.

→

Platform ready to onboard new products and services.

→

New working methodology based on discovery, research and continuous validation.

What followed

The project started as a tech migration and ended up as a product transformation.

The vision was to build a platform ready to evolve alongside the business: a single access point from which clients could manage OCA's services, without depending on multiple systems.

That foundation made it possible to add initiatives like Fulfillment and ME1, prepare the platform for future business verticals, and drive the Ñandú Design System, which I present as independent case studies in this portfolio.
